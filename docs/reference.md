# Reference

Reference is non-actionable information you want to keep. No action required — now or later.

---

## What Reference is

Reference is the part of your system that holds information, not work. Notes, documents, links, records, research — anything you might need to look up later but that doesn't require you to do anything.

The defining question: **does this require any action from me?**

- Yes → it's an Action or a Flow
- No → it's Reference

Reference is not Someday or Wishlist. Someday holds things you intend to do later. Wishlist holds things you might do but haven't committed to. Reference holds things you might look up — no action attached, now or ever.

---

## What lives in Reference

**Evergreen information:**
Notes you've taken, summaries of things you've read, how-to guides, contact information, account credentials, receipts, records.

**Flow support materials:**
Research for an active Flow, meeting notes related to ongoing work, documents connected to a Flow. This is "Flow reference" — it supports active work but isn't itself actionable.

**Vision and Purpose:**
Where you want to be in 3–5 years (Vision), and your core values and reasons for doing what you do (Purpose). These are the two most important things to keep in Reference.

---

## Vision and Purpose

Vision and Purpose are the upper horizons of your system. They don't generate Actions. They don't map to Flows directly. But they're the backdrop against which everything else should make sense.

**Vision** — Your 3–5 year picture. What does your life look like if things go well? What kind of work are you doing? What have you built? Who are you with?

Write it as a description, not a goal list. A goal list is what Goals are for. Vision is a picture. It answers: "If I look back from five years from now, what would make me feel like I lived well?"

**Purpose** — Your core values and principles. Why do you do what you do? What are you ultimately trying to create or contribute?

Purpose rarely changes. It's worth writing down once, putting in Reference, and revisiting annually to see if it still rings true.

**How to use them:**

You don't use Vision and Purpose daily. You don't use them weekly. But once a year — usually at the start of a new year or during a major life transition — you pull them out and ask:

- Do my current Goals reflect my Vision?
- Is the way I'm spending my time consistent with my Purpose?

This is the Annual Review. It's not in the daily or weekly rhythm, but it's what keeps the whole system oriented correctly over time.

---

## How to organize Reference

OTD doesn't prescribe how to organize Reference. The principles are simple:

- **Findable.** If you can't find something when you need it, it's not useful. Whatever organization system makes things findable for you is the right one.
- **Separate from action items.** Reference and actionable work should live in different places, or at least be clearly distinguished. Mixing them creates confusion — you'll scan Reference looking for something and accidentally pick up tasks that belong in Today.
- **Low maintenance.** You shouldn't have to spend significant time curating Reference. File things when they arrive. Retrieve when needed. Occasional pruning during Weekly Review is enough.

---

## Reference vs. Someday vs. Wishlist

Three places for things that aren't in your active system:

- **Reference** — No future action. You might look this up. You will not act on it.
- **Someday** — Deferred commitment. You intend to act on this; the timing is paused. Step 1 is identifiable.
- **Wishlist** — Uncommitted possibility. You might act on this, or you might not. Just an idea, not a plan.

The questions to discriminate:

- "Will I ever need to **do something** with this?" — No → Reference. Yes → Someday or Wishlist.
- "Can I say what step 1 of activation would be?" — Yes → Someday. No → Wishlist.
